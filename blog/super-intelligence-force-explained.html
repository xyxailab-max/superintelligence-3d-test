What Is the Super Intelligence Force? Washington's New AI Task Force, Explained

Use Superintelligence News · Oct 20, 2026

In early October 2026 the word "superintelligence" moved from research papers into the official vocabulary of the U.S. government. Here is a plain-English guide to the new Super Intelligence Force and why it matters.

The announcement

On Sunday, October 4, 2026, President Trump announced on Truth Social that he was creating a federal "Super Intelligence Force." It came days after a White House meeting with the heads of the biggest AI companies, and after he had promised in September to set up an AI task force and name an AI czar.

Who runs it

Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton chairs the group and is effectively the administration's AI, or "SI," czar. Three vice chairs sit alongside him: FTC Chairman Andrew Ferguson, Emil Michael, the Pentagon's under secretary for research and engineering, and Scott Kupor, director of the Office of Personnel Management. The group reports to the President and to White House Chief of Staff Susie Wiles.

What it is supposed to do

  • Keep the U.S. in the lead. Its core job is coordinating federal work so America stays ahead in what the administration now calls super intelligence.
  • Plan for threats. Its charter asks for plans to respond to threats enabled by advanced AI, while avoiding overregulation.
  • Talk to everyone. It is meant to engage consumers, public-interest and religious groups, infrastructure providers and industry.
  • Report in 120 days. The force has a 120-day window to deliver a report on risks and opportunities.

Why "super intelligence" instead of AI?

Shortly before the launch, an executive order renamed AI as "super intelligence" in federal communications. Tech leaders at the White House adopted the term too. The rebrand has pushed searches for the word sharply higher, which is a big reason more people are now looking up what superintelligence actually means.

What it means for you: The force is a coordination body, not a regulator. For now, nothing changes about the AI tools you use day to day, but its 120-day report could shape the rules that come next.

The open questions

Public reporting has not described the force's budget, staff size or formal legal powers. Clayton's stated view is that the biggest danger is the U.S. failing to be first, which signals a focus on speed and competition with China over new restrictions. We will keep updating this page as the force publishes its work.
